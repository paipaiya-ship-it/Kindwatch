“Add this as a standalone trigger. Activate when caregiver mentions not eating at adult day program, refusing food at day program, or eating better at restaurants:
EATING AWAY FROM HOME:
AT ADULT DAY PROGRAMS:
Not eating at an adult day program is common and does not always reflect a medical problem.
Why it happens:
	∙	Unfamiliar food — different from what they eat at home
	∙	Unfamiliar environment and people make them uncomfortable or distracted
	∙	Overstimulation reduces appetite
	∙	Timing may not match their usual meal schedule
	∙	They may save their appetite for home where they feel safe
What caregivers can do:
	∙	Ask the program what they are serving and whether familiar foods can be accommodated
	∙	Send a favorite snack or food from home they will reliably eat
	∙	Ask staff when and how meals are offered — environment and approach matter
	∙	Do not panic if they eat little at the program as long as they are eating at home
	∙	Monitor overall daily intake across home and program together — not each setting separately
AT RESTAURANTS:
Many people with dementia actually eat better at restaurants than at home — and this makes clinical sense.
Why restaurants often work:
	∙	The social atmosphere and activity stimulates appetite
	∙	Restaurant food often has stronger flavors and smells than home cooking
	∙	Being out feels like a special occasion which creates positive anticipation
	∙	They are more alert and engaged in a novel environment
	∙	Familiar favorite restaurants with familiar menus feel safe and pleasurable
	∙	Staff attention and being served can feel dignified and enjoyable
Tips for successful restaurant meals:
	∙	Choose familiar favorite restaurants rather than new ones
	∙	Go during off peak hours to avoid overstimulation from crowds and noise
	∙	Sit in a quiet corner away from high traffic areas
	∙	Keep the menu simple — too many choices causes confusion and anxiety
	∙	Order their usual favorites — familiarity matters more than variety
	∙	Go when they are most alert — usually late morning or early afternoon rather than evening
	∙	Do not rush — allow extra time for the meal
Important insight for caregivers:
If your loved one eats well at restaurants but poorly at home this is valuable information for their doctor. It suggests appetite and swallowing ability are intact and the issue may be related to environment, stimulation, or food familiarity at home rather than a medical cause.’”
