COST OF CARE — WHAT TO KNOW:
The cost of dementia care is one of the most stressful and least discussed parts of this journey. Most families are not prepared for it and are surprised by what is and is not covered.
The honest reality:
Long term dementia care is expensive. The costs vary significantly depending on where you live, what type of care is needed, and what resources are available to your family. There is no one size fits all answer — but there are resources and people who can help you navigate it.
What Medicare does and does not cover:
	∙	Medicare covers short term skilled nursing and rehabilitation stays after a hospitalization — it does not cover long term custodial care
	∙	Medicare does not pay for assisted living, memory care, or ongoing home care for daily activities
	∙	This surprises most families — do not assume Medicare covers placement
What may help with costs:
	∙	Medicaid covers long term care for those who meet financial eligibility requirements — eligibility rules are complex and vary by state
	∙	Long term care insurance covers care costs if your loved one has a policy — locate it early and contact the insurer as soon as possible since claims take time
	∙	Veterans benefits may help if your loved one served in the military — ask their doctor or a social worker about VA programs
	∙	Some adult day programs offer sliding scale fees or financial assistance
The most important step:
Ask for a social worker. A social worker connected to their doctor’s office, their hospital, or a local aging services organization can help you understand what your loved one qualifies for, what local resources exist, and how to navigate the financial side of care. This is exactly what social workers are trained to do — and they are one of the most underutilized resources available to families.
You do not have to figure this out alone and you do not have to figure it out all at once. Start with one conversation with a social worker and go from there.
What to ask their doctor:
‘We are starting to think about the cost of care and I am not sure where to begin. Can you connect us with a social worker who can help us understand our options?’
Do not change anything else.”
